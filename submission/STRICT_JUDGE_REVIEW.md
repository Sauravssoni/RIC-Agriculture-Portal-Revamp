# Strict Evaluator Red-Team Review — RajKisan One

**Lens:** a sceptical Rajasthan Government evaluator who assumes every startup has a modern UI, chatbot, analytics cards and generic API claims.

## Verdict before hardening
A polished portal alone is not defensible. RajKisan Saathi already exists as a strong integrated farmer platform, so a proposal that appears to rebuild RajKisan, invent new source systems or substitute AI for departmental authority should be penalised.

## Attack 1 — “Why does this need to exist when RajKisan already exists?”
**Required answer:** RajKisan One is the Department website’s **public experience contract** over existing transaction rails: discovery, explanation, document readiness, continuity, status readability, feedback, content governance and service observability. Authoritative transactions stay in RajKisan/source systems.

## Attack 2 — “This is just Find → Apply → Track with nicer cards.”
**Required answer:** demonstrate versioned deterministic eligibility, source/version receipts, reuse-first documents, cross-channel resume receipts, service-quality action queues, content freshness governance, GIGW monitoring and explicit production authority states.

## Attack 3 — “The AI chatbot could hallucinate eligibility.”
**Required answer:** **Rules evaluate. AI explains. Government workflows decide.**

## Attack 4 — “Government integrations are fake.”
**Required answer:** connector truth states are explicit: PUBLIC REFERENCE, OFFICIAL SPEC, CONTRACT READY, SANDBOX, ARCHITECTURE ALIGNED. Production onboarding is a pilot gate.

## Attack 5 — “Secure document upload is just a file input.”
**Required answer:** evaluator mode performs allow-list, size and local SHA-256 validation without upload. Production contract specifies malware scan, content-type validation, encryption, short-lived signed access, retention and audit; verified reuse is preferred through authorised eVault/DigiLocker rails.

## Attack 6 — “Analytics is vanity BI.”
**Required answer:** every material friction signal resolves to **evidence → owner → due date → remediation → post-change measurement**.

## Attack 7 — “The portal will become stale again.”
**Required answer:** content lifecycle includes source authority, effective dates, review-by date, Hindi parity, supersession, broken-link checks, content owner and WIM operations.

## Attack 8 — “SUTRA/FarmGraph are scope creep.”
**Required answer:** bounded optional adapters only. SUTRA contributes selective low-connectivity assisted access; FarmGraph contributes advisory/field intelligence patterns. Neither becomes eligibility authority or portal dependency.

## Attack 9 — “You cannot modernise a statewide estate in 90 days.”
**Required answer:** the 90-day ask is a **production-core joint pilot** for selected priority services, controlled connectors, field validation, security/UAT, GIGW readiness and a statewide blueprint.

## Attack 10 — “What measurable success does Rajasthan buy?”
Pilot acceptance must baseline and measure discovery time, task completion/abandonment, document exits, bilingual parity, accessibility defects, content freshness/ownership, connector health, cross-channel continuation and actionable feedback closure.
